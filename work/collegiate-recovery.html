15+ interviews, three themes, and findings to inform how Pennsylvania funds recovery programs on campus.

Pennsylvania leads the nation in collegiate recovery programs, but almost nothing was documented about what actually makes them work, or what gets in the way. The Center for Survey Research conducted 15+ in-depth interviews with staff and leaders of collegiate recovery programs across 5+ organizations statewide on behalf of PA's Department of Drug & Alcohol Programs, aiming to capture the real texture of the work - the funding fights, the space they don't have, and the stigma students still navigate just to walk through the door. Findings were delivered directly to DDAP as an internal deliverable, not released publicly.

Interviewing across Pennsylvania's recovery programs

Participants were recruited from the state's ARHE member list plus additional programs identified through independent research, several of whom held dual roles as both faculty and program leads.

From transcripts to themes

Interview transcripts were coded in NVivo and organized into three sections: program components (peer support, harm reduction, outreach), challenges and obstacles (funding instability, lack of dedicated space, commuter-campus barriers), and stigma (self-stigma and campus culture around substance use).

  • Coauthored a statewide internal report

    Delivered directly to Pennsylvania's Department of Drug & Alcohol Programs, drawing on all 15+ interviews. Findings are internal, not publicly released; happy to discuss in conversation.

  • Findings organized into actionable recommendations

    Dedicated program budgets, allocated physical space, and campus-wide stigma reduction campaigns.

  • Captured a population data alone couldn't reach

    The qualitative approach surfaced patterns - like specific barriers facing commuter-campus programs - that wouldn't have shown up in enrollment numbers.
