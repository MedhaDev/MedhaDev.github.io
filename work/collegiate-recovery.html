Interviewing the people who run Pennsylvania’s collegiate recovery programs, for a report to the state’s Department of Drug and Alcohol Programs.

Pennsylvania leads the nation in collegiate recovery programs, but little was written down about what makes them work or what gets in their way. On behalf of the Department of Drug and Alcohol Programs (DDAP), the Center for Survey Research set out to find out through in-depth interviews with program staff and leaders.

I worked on the interviews and the analysis, and coauthored the report. It went to DDAP as an internal document and wasn’t released publicly, so the findings here are summarized.

Who we talked to

We recruited participants from the state’s Association of Recovery in Higher Education (ARHE) member list, plus programs we found through our own research. Several held two roles at once, as faculty and as program leads.

From transcripts to themes

I coded the transcripts in NVivo and grouped what people told us into three themes:

  • Program components: peer support, harm reduction, outreach.
  • Challenges: unstable funding, no dedicated space, barriers specific to commuter campuses.
  • Stigma: self-stigma, and campus culture around substance use.

What the numbers couldn’t show

Enrollment figures say how many students a program serves, not why it struggles. Talking to the people running these programs surfaced patterns that wouldn’t show up in those numbers, like the specific barriers facing programs on commuter campuses.

What we recommended

The report recommended dedicated program budgets, allocated physical space, and campus-wide campaigns to reduce stigma.
