$284.8 million in crop insurance payouts, and a climate story hiding in the seasonal pattern.

Pennsylvania farmers have been filing more crop insurance claims tied to excess moisture and flooding. Using USDA Risk Management Agency indemnity data via the Southwest Climate Hub's AgRisk Viewer, this brief traced $284.8 million in total crop loss payments from 2017 to 2023, isolating the share specifically attributable to excess moisture and mapping it down to the county level.

Turning indemnity payments into a climate story

Payments were normalized by harvested cropland acreage from the 2022 Census of Agriculture, so counties could be compared on a per-acre basis rather than raw dollar totals. Seasonal analysis showed losses concentrated heavily between April and July, peaking in June.

Mapping the risk

Interactive choropleth maps built in Datawrapper showed Adams, Carbon, Venango, Mercer, and Monroe counties carrying the highest per-acre excess-moisture losses. Crop-level breakdowns found tomatoes hit hardest, with over 70% of their weather-related losses tied to excess moisture specifically.
