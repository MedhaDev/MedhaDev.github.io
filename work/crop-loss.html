A published research brief on what excess moisture costs Pennsylvania farmers, mapped county by county.

Pennsylvania farmers have been filing more crop insurance claims tied to excess moisture and flooding. Using USDA Risk Management Agency indemnity data from the Southwest Climate Hub’s AgRisk Viewer, I traced $284.8 million in crop insurance payments from 2017 to 2023 and worked out how much of it came from excess moisture.

I wrote the brief on my own, from processing the data to the maps and the final text, and the Pennsylvania State Data Center published it in October 2024.

Making counties comparable

Raw dollar totals favor the biggest farming counties, so I divided each county’s payments by its harvested cropland from the 2022 Census of Agriculture. That turns the question into losses per acre, which can be compared across counties of any size.

When and where

Losses were concentrated between April and July, peaking in June. Per acre, Adams, Carbon, Venango, Mercer and Monroe counties had the highest excess-moisture losses, which I mapped in Datawrapper. By crop, tomatoes were hit hardest: more than 70% of their weather-related losses came from excess moisture.

What it recommends

The brief supports investment in drainage infrastructure, wider crop insurance coverage, and climate-resilient crop varieties.
