Making three organizations’ spending comparable for a statewide cost-benefit analysis of autism services.

A statewide autism services initiative wants to know whether its programs are worth what they cost. Answering that means comparing spending across three partner organizations, and each one tracks it differently.

I’m leading the data side of the analysis. It’s still in progress, so results aren’t public yet.

3
partner organizations’ financial data in one model

Three organizations, one cost model

The cost-benefit work started as a data problem. Each partner tracks spending in its own systems, using its own categories, often for the same activity. Before any analysis can happen, someone has to decide how a line item in one organization’s system maps to a line item in another’s, and make that mapping defensible enough that all three trust the comparison. I’m leading that work, including integrating data from the partners’ ERP systems.
